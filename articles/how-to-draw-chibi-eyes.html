Chibi Art Tutorial

How to Draw Chibi Eyes — Step-by-Step with Clip Studio Paint

Chibi eyes are usually larger and simpler than realistic eyes, but they still carry most of the character's emotion. A reliable workflow is to build the eye from a clean outer shape, then add iris, pupil, highlights and color in layers.

1. Start with the expression

Decide whether the character looks cheerful, calm, shy, surprised or angry. The upper eyelid angle and eyebrow position should support that emotion before you add detail.

2. Draw a simple outer eye shape

Use a thicker upper lash line and a lighter lower edge. Avoid overworking the lower lid; too much detail can make a chibi face look older or more realistic than intended.

3. Place the iris and pupil

Large irises make the eye feel cute and expressive. Keep the pupil and iris centered according to the direction of the gaze.

4. Add value before color

Block a darker upper area and a lighter lower area. This creates depth even with a simple palette. In Clip Studio Paint, separate line art, base color, shadow and highlight layers so each stage remains editable.

5. Use highlights deliberately

One large highlight plus one or two smaller accents is usually enough. Too many highlights can reduce readability, especially when the character will be used as an avatar or emote.

6. Check both eyes together

Zoom out often. The two eyes should share the same visual style even when perspective or expression changes their shape.

Practical rule: the face must still read clearly at thumbnail size. Simplify details that disappear when zoomed out.
